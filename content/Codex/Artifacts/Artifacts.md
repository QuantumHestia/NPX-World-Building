# Artifacts of Eldoria

> [!abstract]
> **Archival Definition**
>
> An artifact is, in-of-itself, a working whose claim has outlived its original act of casting.
>
> Some artifacts persist through construction. Others are sustained by divine authority, inherited obligation, place-accord, repeated observance, or so much recognition that removing the object would not remove what it has come to mean.

Artifacts have shaped dynasties, wars, migrations, cults, and laws. Their rarity does not make every legend about them true. It makes reliable inspection difficult and forgery profitable.

> [!archivist-note-right]
> Age is not evidence of wisdom. It merely gives a mistake more time to acquire witnesses.

---

## How the Archive Classifies an Artifact

Every complete dossier should record:

- **Claim** — what reality the artifact asserts.
- **Authority** — why the Weave admits that claim.
- **Form** — how a bearer activates or directs it.
- **Anchor** — what keeps its effects coherent.
- **Burden** — who or what sustains the contradiction.
- **Recognition history** — what institutions, traditions, or fears have made the artifact mean.
- **Settlement profile** — how failure, overuse, or unpaid burden is likely to manifest.

Cross-reference: [[Codex/Systems/The-Magic-System/The-Unified-Theory-of-the-Weave|The Unified Theory of the Weave]].

# The Regalia of the First Men

The **Crown of Summer**, **Robes of Autumn**, **Sword of Winter**, and **Staff of Spring** are collectively known as the **Regalia of the First Men**.

**[Record | Medium confidence]** Human royal and temple traditions consistently attribute the four artifacts to [[Codex/NPCs/Factions/The-Aegis-Court/Demeter|Demeter]]. In those accounts, the goddess entrusted them to early human communities during a long war with Elven forces.

**[Claim | Disputed]** Later human chronicles describe the Elves as the aggressors and credit the Regalia as the sole cause of humanity’s victory. The surviving record does not establish either claim. Elven accounts are fragmentary, withheld, or preserved under names the Archive has not confidently matched to the same conflict.

The Regalia were probably more important than four powerful weapons. Together they formed a model for keeping a people alive through catastrophe:

| Season | Office | Governing principle |
|---|---|---|
| **Spring** | The Tender | Renewal must begin from something still living. |
| **Summer** | The Sovereign | Strength must be given a shared direction. |
| **Autumn** | The Steward | What is gathered must be preserved, divided, and sometimes surrendered. |
| **Winter** | The Warden | What cannot continue must be allowed—or compelled—to end. |

> [!archivist-note-left]
> Human epics call them weapons because epics understand battles better than granaries. Armies also die from spoiled grain, broken command, untreated wounds, and wars no one knows how to finish.

## The Crown of Summer

### Recorded Description

The Crown is described as a circlet of hammered gold bearing four rays rather than jewels. In sunlight, inscriptions become visible along its inner edge. No surviving transcription records the same words twice.

### Working Claim

> **Those who stand beneath one sun may act as one people.**

The Crown does not, and can not grant absolute obedience. It gives a recognized leader the ability to make their meaning understood across distance, noise, fear, and differences of language. Those who freely accept the bearer’s authority can coordinate with unnatural clarity and resist panic, confusion, and hostile compulsion.

### Authority and Anchor

- **Authority:** Demeter’s patronage and the public recognition of the bearer’s office.
- **Form:** The bearer must address a named community beneath open daylight.
- **Anchor:** A reciprocal declaration: the people recognize the bearer, and the bearer accepts responsibility for them.

### Burden and Limitations

The Crown cannot manufacture legitimate authority. A bearer who claims people they do not know, protect, or acknowledge creates a weak and unstable anchor.

While the Crown is active, the bearer cannot exclude themselves from the needs they impose upon others. Hunger, forced marches, sleeplessness, and exposure suffered by the recognized community increasingly manifest upon the bearer.

### Likely Settlements

- **Entrenchment:** Successful use can turn temporary command into dynastic legitimacy.
- **Substitution:** Ambiguous commands may be fulfilled according to the community’s shared understanding rather than the bearer’s private intent.
- **Attractor Attention:** Gods and powers associated with sovereignty, sunlight, conquest, or civic order can recognize its use.

### Current Record

Three royal houses display objects they identify as the Crown of Summer. None has permitted an activation audit before a politically neutral assembly.

The Archive therefore recognizes **three claimants and no authenticated Crown**.

## The Robes of Autumn

### Recorded Description

Accounts describe layered robes woven in rust, ochre, brown, and the dark red of late fruit. Fallen leaves embroidered along the hem change species according to the land in which the garment is worn.

### Working Claim

> **What has been gathered will not be wasted.**

The Robes preserve food, seed, medicine, bodies awaiting burial, and wounded people who would otherwise decline before treatment. They can slow rot, blood loss, spoilage, and the collapse of failing wards.

The Robes do not create abundance. They preserve what has already been grown, made, learned, or saved.

### Authority and Anchor

- **Authority:** Demeter’s authority over harvest, stewardship, and the boundary between ripeness and decay.
- **Form:** The bearer inventories what will be preserved and names those for whom it is kept.
- **Anchor:** A real store, community, or body of gathered resources under the bearer’s care.

### Burden and Limitations

Preservation delays an ending; it does not abolish one. The bearer must identify what may be consumed, distributed, planted, buried, or released when the preservation ends.

The Robes grow heavier as they hold more unresolved decay. If never allowed to lie fallow, they preserve too broadly: fruit refuses to ripen, wounds refuse to scar, grief refuses to soften, and institutions refuse to admit they have failed.

### Likely Settlements

- **Reversion:** Neglected stores may age all at once when the Robes’ support is withdrawn.
- **Compensation:** Unnamed waste or spoilage may settle into another part of the protected store.
- **Entrenchment:** The bearer can acquire lasting authority over rationing, inheritance, and burial customs.

### Current Record

The last credible sighting occurred during the Famine of Seven Granaries. Records agree that one storehouse fed an entire district for a winter. They disagree about why every seed planted there the following spring failed to germinate.

## The Sword of Winter

### Recorded Description

The Sword is said to be pale, undecorated, and cold only at the moment it is drawn. Frost forms upon nearby thresholds; old wounds, sealed letters, and objects associated with unfinished obligations.

### Working Claim

> **What has reached its ending will not be forced to continue.**

The Sword is less a weapon of cold than an instrument of closure. It can sever magical bindings, end regeneration, close unstable breaches, break an occupying spirit’s claim, and impose finality upon effects that survive only because no one has successfully concluded them.

### Authority and Anchor

- **Authority:** Demeter’s winter aspect: dormancy, necessary death, and the boundary that permits later renewal.
- **Form:** The wielder must name what is ending and accept that the same claim may not simply be restored afterward.
- **Anchor:** A genuine point of closure—a defeated foe, fulfilled term, broken covenant, completed life, or exhausted working.

### Burden and Limitations

The Sword cannot safely end what the wielder merely dislikes. Used against something still sustained by a valid claim, it may sever the nearest related bond instead.

Its workings are difficult to reverse even by the bearer. Every use therefore risks ending alliances, protections, memories, or obligations connected to the named target.

### Likely Settlements

- **Substitution:** If the intended ending is invalid, the blade finds the nearest legitimate closure.
- **Compensation:** A severed covenant may transfer its unresolved terms to guarantors, descendants, or territory.
- **Attractor Attention:** Authorities of death, law, winter, and sealed thresholds are unusually sensitive to its use.

### Current Record

Human ballads place the Sword in a royal burial vault. One Elven source claims it was sealed beneath the White Forest because its last bearer attempted to “end the war” and instead ended the road home.

Neither site has been verified.

## The Staff of Spring

### Recorded Description

The Staff is described as living wood that buds in response to nearby injuries. Its leaves vary by account: ash in military chronicles, apple in farming hymns, oak in royal genealogies, and an unnamed silver-leafed tree in Demeter’s oldest rites.

### Working Claim

> **What remains viable may begin again.**

The Staff awakens dormant seed, accelerates natural recovery, restores exhausted soil, coaxes poisoned land toward health, and reinforces a living pattern that has not yet passed beyond recovery.

It cannot return the dead, recreate an erased identity, or restore what has no viable pattern from which to grow.

### Authority and Anchor

- **Authority:** Demeter’s patronage over fertility, cultivation, and renewed growth.
- **Form:** The bearer must provide seed, living tissue, uncontaminated soil, or another real beginning.
- **Anchor:** Whatever viable pattern remains within the subject.

### Burden and Limitations

The Staff accelerates a future the subject could still possess. It does not make that future free.

Rapid healing consumes food, sleep, and bodily reserves. Rapid growth consumes water and soil fertility. Repeated restoration without recovery produces early flowering followed by exhaustion, sterility, or blight.

### Likely Settlements

- **Compensation:** Growth draws necessary substance from the bearer, surrounding land, or supplied stores.
- **Reversion:** Restoration fails if the surviving pattern is too damaged or loses its anchor.
- **Substitution:** Poorly defined renewal may restore an older, wilder, or unintended form.

### Current Record

The priesthood of Demeter in [[Codex/Settings/Kingdoms/The Summer Isles/Cities/Sarnoît|Sarnoît]] carries a budding staff during Harvest-tide and identifies it as the original relic. The staff has never been tested outside ceremonial conditions.

The possibility that it is a sanctioned ritual successor—not the original object—remains open.

## The Regalia as a Complete Cycle

When used in sequence, the Regalia distribute a society’s burdens across a complete seasonal pattern:

1. **Spring begins** what remains possible.
2. **Summer unites** people behind the work.
3. **Autumn preserves and apportions** what the work produces.
4. **Winter concludes** what cannot or should not be carried forward.

Their most dangerous uses occur when one principle is isolated from the others:

- Spring without Winter creates growth without endings.
- Summer without Autumn consumes people faster than they can recover.
- Autumn without Spring preserves a society that can no longer renew itself.
- Winter without Summer ends claims without communal authority to decide what follows.

**[Inference | Medium confidence]** The four bearers may originally have been offices rather than champions: Tender, Sovereign, Steward, and Warden. Later monarchies collapsed these roles into the image of one heroic ruler because shared authority is inconvenient to dynastic history.

> [!archivist-note-right]
> If one person in a heroic song wears all four relics, assume the poet was paid by that person’s descendants.

# Additional Artifact Leads

The following concepts already appear in surviving records or warrant dedicated investigation.

## Referenced Relics Awaiting Dossiers

- **The Aegis** — a legitimating and protective relic associated with divine patronage, favored bloodlines, and sanctioned cities.
- **The Mirror of Eternity** — attributed to Apollo and said to reveal possible futures or truths the viewer has concealed from themselves.
- **The Orb of Measures** — a temple relic associated with the moral or symbolic weight of decisions rather than direct prophecy.

## Proposed Archival Dossiers

- **The Hearthspark of Orra** — a cantrip-scale flame that became the anchor of civic sanctuary through centuries of hospitality law.
- **The Mercy Needle** — a healing implement that draws injury into glass thread instead of pretending the burden has vanished.
- **The Key of the Unwalked Road** — a passage relic that shortens travel while retaining the metaphysical debt of the omitted distance.
- **The Bell Without a Tongue** — a warning bell heard only by people who have already crossed the boundary it protects.
- **The Empty Scabbard** — a sheath that can disarm any named weapon once, but thereafter recognizes that weapon as its rightful occupant.
- **The Last Seed of a Dead Country** — a seed containing the viable pattern of an extinct landscape, dangerous because restoration would overwrite whatever now grows there.

# Open Questions

- Were the Regalia gifts, bargains, inherited offices, or spoils later attributed to Demeter?
- Was there one Elven war or several conflicts compressed into a single heroic age?
- Did the four artifacts require four simultaneous bearers?
- Are the claimed modern objects originals, ritual successors, deliberate copies, or political forgeries?
- What obligation did the first human communities accept in exchange for Demeter’s authority?
- What happened the last time all four Regalia were assembled?

Until those questions are answered, the Archive records the tradition without endorsing the triumphal history built around it.
